12.2.5 Remote management of hypertensive patients

Regions should gradually establish information systems for managing chronic diseases such as hypertension and create remote management platforms for hypertensive patients. By using electronic blood pressure monitors with remote transmission capabilities, patients can monitor their blood pressure data from home and receive guidance from doctors without needing to visit the clinic. This allows for dynamic blood pressure management and improves the quality of care. Additionally, establishing a remote consultation system and data transmission system within medical alliances, based on information platforms, can effectively increase the rate of blood pressure control in hypertensive patients, and reduce the incidence and mortality of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases.[753]

12.2.6 Team Building and Facility, Medication Provision

Primary healthcare institutions should form hypertension management teams centered around general practitioners, providing standardized management services to hypertensive patients through contracted services. These institutions should also be equipped with appropriate hypertension management devices, including certified upper arm electronic blood pressure monitors, height and weight scales, measuring tapes, blood routine analyzers, urine routine analyzers, blood biochemistry analyzers, electrocardiograph machines, and ambulatory blood pressure monitors. Primary healthcare institutions with the necessary resources should also be equipped with cardiac ultrasound machines, vascular color Doppler ultrasound, chest X-ray equipment, and fundus examination devices. Additionally, a complete supply of antihypertensive medications should be available to ensure seamless integration with higher-level hospitals.

Family-based standardized management of hypertensive patients involves both the patients and their family members. 13 Internet-Based Medical Care for Hypertension

With the continuous integration of internet technology and the healthcare field, internet-based medical care for hypertension has developed significantly, offering complementary and advanced methods to traditional hypertension management. This approach is also an essential tool for achieving the hypertension-related goals outlined in the “Healthy China 2030” initiative.

13.1 Research Evidence in Internet-Based Medical Care for Hypertension

Internet-based medical care for hypertension began with remote blood pressure monitoring. As early as 1996, American scholars reported on the use of telephone-based remote home blood pressure monitoring to improve patient compliance and control rates. Compared to the conventional observation group, the remote monitoring group showed a reduction in clinic systolic/diastolic blood pressure by 4.7/4.4 mmHg.[754]

In 2008, the application of “Internet medical care” in hypertension management was explored through randomized controlled trials involving electronic communication and home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM).[755] The study aimed to assess whether combining internet-based services, HBPM, and pharmacist consultations could enhance blood pressure control rates. The results demonstrated that the group receiving remote blood pressure monitoring, online education, and pharmacist consultation had a significantly higher blood pressure control rate (56%) compared to the remote monitoring plus online education group (36%) and the conventional observation group (31%). The mean reduction in systolic/diastolic blood pressure was 13.2/4.6 mmHg more in the remote monitoring plus pharmacist consultation group than in the conventional observation group.[756]

In 2021, Japanese researchers published the results of the HERB-digital hypertension study, which developed the HERB system to establish a communication platform between patients and healthcare providers. This system included an application for hypertension patients and a web application for healthcare providers. Compared to the standard lifestyle intervention group, the group receiving HERB system guidance combined with standard lifestyle interventions showed an additional reduction in 24-h ambulatory blood pressure, home blood pressure, and clinic systolic blood pressure by 2.4, 4.3, and 3.6 mmHg, respectively, at 12 weeks. This additional antihypertensive effect was maintained at 24-week follow-up in patients taking antihypertensive medications.[757] In the same year, British researchers released a study on home and online blood pressure management, which tested the effectiveness of digital interventions for hypertension by combining self-monitoring with guided self-management through mobile health management software. After one year, the internet intervention group experienced a reduction in average blood pressure from 151.7/86.4 mmHg to 138.4/80.2 mmHg, compared to a reduction from 151.6/85.3 mmHg to 141.8/79.8 mmHg in the conventional observation group.[758] In Tianjin, China, a randomized controlled study was conducted on elderly individuals aged 60-79 years, involving 252 patients with poorly controlled blood pressure. The participants were randomly assigned to either a remote home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) group, which established a communication platform between doctors and patients, or a conventional management group. After 15 months of management, the remote HBPM group demonstrated a significantly higher rate of home blood pressure control (home blood pressure < 135/85 mmHg) compared to the conventional management group (71.3% vs. 49.8%, P < 0.01), with this difference emerging as early as the third month of management.[759]

A meta-analysis of 23 randomized controlled trials on remote HBPM showed that compared to conventional observation, remote HBPM significantly improved the control of clinic systolic/diastolic blood pressure (mean difference -4.71/-2.45 mmHg, P < 0.001), with an increase in blood pressure control rates by 16%.[760] In another meta-analysis that included 46 randomized controlled trials on remote HBPM, the remote HBPM group experienced a greater reduction in systolic and diastolic blood pressure by an average of 3.99/1.99 mmHg compared to the conventional observation group. When remote HBPM was combined with consultation, lifestyle education, behavioral management, and medication supervision, the average reduction in systolic/diastolic blood pressure was further enhanced by 2.44/1.12 mmHg.[761] These studies had follow-up periods ranging from 6 to 15 months, and whether there are longer-term benefits remains to be confirmed through extended follow-up studies.

13.2 Advantages of Internet-Based Medical Care for Hypertension

Remote blood pressure monitoring offers several advantages, particularly in the screening, diagnosis, and management of hypertension. These benefits are further enhanced when combined with multiple interventions, including the involvement of professional doctors, nurses, or pharmacists, lifestyle and risk factor control education for hypertension patients, medication advice, and supervision. The potential additional effects of remote blood pressure monitoring include improved treatment compliance, optimized medication use, enhanced patient quality of life, and reduced risk of cardiovascular complications.[762]

Moreover, many individuals are unable to visit hospitals regularly for follow-up consultations. This digital remote approach enables general practitioners or specialists to manage chronic diseases more effectively within communities while providing a more convenient option for many hypertension patients, thus reducing the frequency of hospital visits.

13.3 Exploration of Internet-Based Medical Care for Hypertension in China

In China, continuous exploration in the field of internet-based medical care for hypertension has led to the establishment of various patient-provider interaction platforms.[763] Furthermore, mobile health applications utilizing wearable devices or smartwatches combined with machine learning models are particularly important for developing efficient remote blood pressure monitoring solutions in the future, providing effective decision support for clinicians.

Internet-based medical care requires the construction of an information infrastructure that integrates data from both inside and outside hospitals, encompassing blood pressure monitoring, data upload, feedback, consultations, guidance, prescriptions, education, follow-up, and other activities, ultimately forming a closed-loop management system for blood pressure. This approach not only aids in managing hypertension but is also expected to soon transform traditional hypertension management models. However, further research is needed to confirm the benefits concerning hard endpoints and long-term outcomes.

Additionally, following the guidelines set out in the “Opinions on Promoting the Development of Internet + Healthcare”[764] and the “Administrative Measures for Internet Hospitals (Provisional)”,[765] the scope of internet-based medical care for hypertension includes follow-up visits, prescription circulation, HBPM, health management services, and lifestyle guidance and interventions. Issues such as data privacy and security, technical challenges, responsibility allocation, and internet medical service fees must also be addressed in parallel. Currently, out-of-hospital internet medical care remains in its early stages, and it is crucial to optimize resource allocation and establish a tightly linked hypertension diagnosis and treatment management system that connects in-hospital and out-of-hospital services, as well as online and offline care.

14 HYPERTENSION PREVENTION AND CONTROL POLICIES AND HEALTHCARE SERVICE SYSTEM
14.1 Prevention and Control Policies

Hypertension, while largely preventable and controllable, is difficult to cure. Since 2017, China has implemented a series of policies targeting chronic disease management.

(1) “Healthy China 2030” planning outline: this is the first long-term strategic plan in the health sector at the national level in China. It establishes a “Big Health” and “Big Hygiene” concept focused on promoting health across the entire lifespan. Under this guidance, the “Healthy China Action (2019-2030)” includes cardiovascular disease prevention actions with goals set for 2022 and 2030. These goals include reducing cardiovascular mortality to 209.7 per 100,000 and 190.7 per 100,000 by 2022 and 2030, respectively. Additionally, the awareness rate of hypertension among residents aged 30 and above should reach at least 55% by 2022 and 65% by 2030. The standardized management rate of hypertension patients should not be less than 60% and 70%, respectively, with continuous improvement in treatment and control rates. Additionally, societal and governmental requirements include the comprehensive implementation of blood pressure measurement at the first medical visit for individuals aged 35 and above. Primary healthcare institutions are expected to provide standardized health management services to residents aged 35 and older within their jurisdiction who have been diagnosed with essential hypertension. Moreover, there is an ongoing effort to promote the integrated management of the “three highs” (hypertension, hyperglycemia, and hyperlipidemia), including the assessment and intervention guidance for high-risk populations with overweight/obesity, elevated blood pressure, elevated blood sugar, and abnormal lipid profiles. The standardized management of hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia is a key focus of these initiatives.

(2) “Medium and Long-term Plan for Chronic Disease Prevention and Control in China (2017-2025)”: this plan provides a long-term framework for chronic disease prevention and control in China. By 2025, it aims to achieve effective control of chronic disease risk factors and establish comprehensive, life-long health management for the entire population, with the goal of reducing the premature mortality rate from cardiovascular diseases, cancer, chronic respiratory diseases, and diabetes in people aged 30 to 70 by 20% from 2015 levels.

(3) “Guidelines on Promoting the Construction of a Tiered Diagnosis and Treatment System”: This policy proposes a tiered diagnosis and treatment model characterized by initial diagnosis at the community level, bidirectional referrals, differentiated management of acute and chronic conditions, and coordinated interaction between different levels of healthcare institutions.

(4) “Notice on the Management of Long-Term Prescriptions for Chronic Diseases”: This notice encourages the development of policies that allow long-term prescription management for chronic disease patients. It outlines the permissible duration of prescriptions, the range of medicines, and the safety protocols to reduce the frequency of medication refills for stable chronic disease patients. For chronic disease patients with stable conditions, prescriptions can be issued for up to 12 weeks of medication at a time. Remote guidance should be strengthened through various means, such as telephone follow-ups. Additionally, patient education on medication use should be enhanced for those using long-term prescriptions, with the aim of improving self-management and medication adherence.

(5) “Opinions of the General Office of the State Council on Promoting the Development of Internet + Healthcare”: This document introduced a series of policy measures, clearly demonstrating strong support for the development of 'Internet + Healthcare.' The document emphasizes a policy orientation that encourages innovation while maintaining a balanced, prudent approach. It also delineates the regulatory and safety boundaries. This policy is designed to help alleviate the disparities in healthcare development and to meet the increasingly diverse healthcare needs of the population.

(6) The Latest “14th Five-Year Plan”: The plan makes detailed provisions for the chronic disease management industry, prioritizing the establishment of a public health system, enhancing public health service projects, strengthening chronic disease prevention, early screening, and comprehensive interventions, and improving the mental health service system.

14.2 Healthcare Service System

China's healthcare service system consists of three levels: (1) community-based primary healthcare system: this is the first tier, which provides basic health services; (2) community general clinics: this second tier includes community-based general practice clinics; (3) urban comprehensive hospitals: the third tier encompasses large urban hospitals that offer specialized care.

The hypertension prevention and control service system is embedded within the “National Basic Public Health Service Standards”, emphasizing systematic management of hypertension. This approach includes the provision of fair and continuous screening, diagnosis, treatment, referral, and long-term follow-up services to all residents. The healthcare service system, along with the general population, shares the responsibility for hypertension management. Key aspects include: (1) integration into local health service policies: this includes: (a) providing financial support for detection and management strategies tailored to the local prevalence of hypertension and economic conditions, as well as offering favorable policies for medication treatment; (b) supporting regular training for community doctors within the service area and allowing trained non-clinical staff, such as nurses and pharmacists, to participate in hypertension screening and lifestyle guidance; (c) ensuring a smooth bidirectional referral pathway for patients with complex or resistant hypertension; and (d) making the quality and effectiveness of hypertension prevention and treatment a primary evaluation metric in the performance assessments of healthcare institutions at all levels.

(2) Lifelong Management of Hypertension: Once hypertension occurs, it requires lifelong management. Effective management is key to preventing serious cardiovascular complications. Primary healthcare providers are the first line of defense in hypertension prevention and control, and they must take on the primary responsibility for detection, registration, treatment, and long-term systematic management.

(3) Establishing a Unified, Digitalized Cardiovascular Disease Management and Expert Consultation Network: A unified, networked electronic health service information management system can significantly improve the efficiency of hypertension treatment and management. Additionally, a cardiovascular disease expert consultation network can provide continuing education for primary care providers and offer immediate guidance to patients, thereby improving the management of hypertension.

(4) Implementation of Evidence-Based Healthcare Policies: Policies should be based on medical research evidence, with service quality and outcomes as indicators and community-wide cardiovascular health as the goal. These policies should include monitoring and evaluation systems based on evidence, and the results should guide resource allocation and personnel decisions within the healthcare system.

The “National Standards and Specifications for Hospital Informatization Construction (Trial)” issued by the National Health Commission in 2018 focused on the integration of medical care and prevention. In recent years, the government has promoted the establishment of regional medical centers at all levels, advanced the standardization of medical consortia, and supported the construction of close-knit county-level healthcare service communities. As public expectations for healthcare services continue to rise, it is essential to build a high-quality and efficient integrated healthcare service system to meet the comprehensive, life-long health needs of the population.

15 RESEARCH OUTLOOK

Based on a systematic review of the existing literature in the field of hypertension and related diseases, the following suggestions are proposed for future research directions and content: (1) leveraging emerging technologies: fully utilize emerging technologies such as internet-based healthcare, wearable devices, and artificial intelligence to explore and optimize hypertension management models that are tailored to China's national conditions.

(2) Targeted early prevention and cardiovascular management: conduct early prevention and comprehensive cardiovascular disease management for specific populations, such as workplace employees and individuals undergoing health check-ups.

(3) Research on Treatment Thresholds and Targets: Further investigate the initial treatment thresholds and blood pressure targets for specific patients (e.g., ISH patients, individuals aged 80 years and above, particularly those who are frail, low-to-moderate cardiovascular risk patients, diabetics, and those with CKD). Ongoing research, such as the “Intervention for High-Normal Blood Pressure in Type 2 Diabetes: A Randomized Controlled Trial (IMPACT)”, will clarify whether the timing of blood pressure lowering in diabetic patients should be advanced.

(4) Ambulatory and Home Blood Pressure Monitoring: Further clarify the treatment thresholds and blood pressure targets for initiating treatment based on ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) and home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM).

(5) Optimal Blood Pressure Levels Across Life Stages: Investigate the optimal systolic and diastolic blood pressure levels at different stages of life.

(6) Short- and Long-Term Blood Pressure Variability: Evaluate whether short-term blood pressure variability (BPV) and long-term BPV should be considered in cardiovascular risk assessments.

(7) Achieving and Evaluating Sustained Blood Pressure Control: Explore how to achieve and assess sustained, long-term blood pressure control, and identify which treatment regimens are most effective in improving long-term BPV and time in therapeutic range (TTR), thereby offering greater cardiovascular benefits.

(8) Blood Pressure and Cardiovascular and Renal Outcomes in Youth: Investigate the association between blood pressure levels in children and adolescents and cardiovascular and renal outcomes in later life.

(9) Correlation Between Lifetime Blood Pressure Trajectories and Hypertension Phenotypes with Cardiovascular and Renal Outcomes: Investigate how the trajectory of blood pressure throughout life and different hypertension phenotypes are associated with cardiovascular and renal outcomes.

(10) Impact of Medication-Induced Nocturnal Blood Pressure Reduction on Clinical Outcomes: Study the effects of reducing nighttime blood pressure through medication on clinical outcomes.

(11) Effect of Antihypertensive Treatment on Clinical Outcomes in Patients with White-Coat Hypertension and Masked Hypertension: Assess the impact of antihypertensive treatment on clinical outcomes in patients with white-coat hypertension and masked hypertension.

(12) Impact of Pharmacological Treatment on Cardiovascular and Renal Events in Truly Resistant Hypertension: Evaluate the effects of drug treatment on cardiovascular and renal events in patients with truly resistant hypertension.

(13) Impact of Renal Denervation (RDN) on Cardiovascular and Renal Outcomes: Investigate how renal denervation affects cardiovascular and renal outcomes.

(14) Efficacy and Application of Cuffless Blood Pressure Monitoring Devices: Assess the effectiveness and potential applications of cuffless blood pressure monitoring devices.

(15) How to Effectively Assess and Improve Treatment Adherence: Explore methods for effectively evaluating treatment adherence and interventions to improve adherence.
